The workflow

  1. 1Order A contractor sends a spoken or typed order in everyday shop language. ShopFlow maps it to real catalogue SKUs — and asks when a request is ambiguous instead of guessing.
  2. 2Verify Each line is checked against live inventory to find exactly what is short.
  3. 3Quote A clean quotation the owner can read out or share straight away.
  4. 4Supplier intelligence A photographed dealer price list is read and compared against what the shop paid before, surfacing increases that would otherwise pass unnoticed.
  5. 5Budget The owner states the cash actually available. That constraint is real, and it binds.
  6. 6Decision A purchase plan: what to buy now, what to defer, and what deferring risks. Customer commitments come first.
  7. 7Why Every recommendation opens up into the full calculation behind it — stock, velocity, lead time, margin, budget remaining.

Steps 1–3 and 7 are live above. Steps 4–6 are built and tested in the deterministic engine, and reach the interface next.

The trust principle

The AI never invents business numbers.

The language model understands the order, chooses which tool to call and asks for clarification. Every quantity, price, shortage and total is produced by a separate deterministic engine that cannot reach a model. SKU ids are rejected unless they exist in the catalogue, and any figure the model writes is checked against the engine's output before it is shown. That separation is enforced by the architecture and covered by tests, not asserted in a prompt.
